## The story

**The candidates, checked on 2026-10-08.** Open gaps move; the check date is part of the brief. On that date MLX's `good first issue` label had zero open issues and its `performance` label had 13. Five candidates survive the "is it still open" test, in order of how much of the course each one uses.

**1. A chunkwise prefill kernel for the gated delta rule, in Metal.** Week 3 day 3's open problem. All three Metal kernels in mlx-lm's `gated_delta.py` still walk the sequence one token at a time, `for (int t = 0; t < T; ++t)`, at three places in the file. An open pull request, #1870, adds a chunkwise version at the ops level for training, "scalar gating without a mask takes a new `gated_delta_chunkwise`", and its table shows why the sequential form hurts at scale: "4096 | 108.33 GB | 39.02 s" for the backward ([mlx-lm PR #1870](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/pull/1870)). A new issue, #1956, asks for "a chunked (WY / parallel) formulation like the one in flash-linear-attention". The Metal kernel does not exist. The trap is week 3 day 3: the baseline is the register-resident scan, not a Python loop, and the May attempt lost to it past 1,024 tokens. A brief for this one must say what the chunked kernel does on an Apple core that the scan cannot, and the answer is the tiled matrix multiply from week 2 day 3 feeding the neural accelerators from week 3 day 2.

**2. The expert matvec at batch one.** MLX issue #4509, open: "gather_qmm at M=1 streams 4-bit expert weights at ~half the bandwidth of M=4 (MoE decode)", where "the routed-expert matmuls are ~40% of each decode step", measured at "151 GB/s" on an M1 Max ([issue #4509](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/issues/4509)). Week 2 day 5's regimes and week 3 day 4's expert kernels apply directly. No pull request targets it. The brief's bound is simple: the kernel reads expert weights once, so the floor is bytes over bandwidth, and the gap to close is a factor of two.

**3. Attention backward with an array mask.** The fused backward merged on 2026-09-29 "For the causal case we have up to 2.8x speedups. Memory improves in every configuration, up to 24x at the longest shape: 24.63 GiB to 1 GiB" ([MLX PR #4563](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/pull/4563)). The source still says "the backward pass does not support an array mask; only causal masking is implemented." Week 2 day 4's kernel, one case short. No open issue or pull request.

**4. A correctness starter.** Issue #4624, open: norms and softmax drop rows when the grid is wider than 2^32, "Of R rows, only ((R × tg) mod 2^32) / tg size rows are actually computed". Small, exact, and a test of whether your contract catches boundaries.

**5. Quantized matmul at prefill sizes.** Issue #4621, open: "quantized_matmul takes 14- 41% longer than dequantize + bf16 matmul at prefill sizes (> 512 tokens)". The large-M regime from week 2 day 5, where the quantized kernel should be a matmul and is not keeping up with one.

**Two that closed while this course was written.** The small-batch quantized matvec gap was narrowed by two merges, on 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-06. The fp32 small-N matmul in issue #4571 got a pull request, "Add thin M GEMM dispatch", on 2026-10-08. Do not pick either without re-checking, and re-check whichever one you do pick, on the day.

**What to write.** One file, `weeks/w4/brief.md`, with the seven parts from yesterday, for one candidate. Concretely:

1. The shape, with the model and the dtype. For candidate 1, the Qwen3.6 shape from PR #1559: batch 1, 16 key heads, 32 value heads, head size 128, sequence 2,048 and 8,192.
2. The three questions, answered. For candidate 1, the fast path to check is whether MLX core's new NAX gated-delta kernels already cover prefill.
3. The bound: count the bytes and operations for the chunked form at chunk size 64, place it on the M5's roofline, and give the floor in microseconds. Use week 2 day 3's intensity formula.
4. The baseline: the register-resident scan kernel, timed interleaved, at both sequence lengths.
5. The contract: a float64 reference built from the sequential rule; a stated tolerance, because chunking changes summation order and cannot be bit-exact; test inputs at the chunk boundary and at the first and last token; bit-exactness across repeated runs.
6. The output: generated source via `verbose=True`, time against the floor, and the end-to-end prefill number, because week 3 day 3 showed a 2x kernel can be 6 percent of the model.
7. The kill rule: if the kernel is slower than the scan at 2,048 tokens after two rounds of tile changes, stop and publish the negative result with the mechanism.

No code. If a sentence in the brief would need an AI to guess, it is not finished.
