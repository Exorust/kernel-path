# Week 6, day 2 · Megakernels

**Why this day.** Week 3 day 5 set up the launch-overhead problem. This is the loudest answer to it, with the headline numbers, the independent check that cut the headline down, and the regime where it still wins.

## The story

**The claim.** In May 2025 Hazy Research fused the whole forward pass of a one-billion-parameter model into one GPU kernel. Their numbers, for "a 32-token prompt and 128 generated tokens, with no speculation": "on an H100, we use 78% of memory bandwidth and outperform existing systems by over 1.5x", where "vLLM and SGLang – are only able to use at most 50% of available GPU bandwidth when running this workload on an H100". "On an H100, our megakernel runs almost 2.5x faster than vLLM and over 1.5x faster than SGLang. On a B200, the gap with vLLM rises to over 3.5x", with "under 680 microseconds per forward pass" on the B200 ([Hazy Research, No Bubbles](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-27-no-bubbles)). The mechanism is week 3 day 5: "existing systems break down a model forward pass into around a hundred separate kernels", and each boundary leaves the GPU idle. One more mechanism, the tail: "if a kernel runs 512 thread blocks (like our Llama-1B down projection), but we only have 148 streaming multiprocessors (like on a B200), we end up with 80 empty SM's at the end." A kernel's last wave does not fill the machine, and in a chain of a hundred kernels that happens a hundred times. The baselines "use CUDA graphs and torch compilation", so the win is on top of the standard fix.

**The check.** In 2026 a third party rebuilt Hazy's kernel as 81 ordinary kernels connected by CUDA graphs and programmatic dependent launch, which lets a kernel start before the previous one has fully drained. "On an NVIDIA H100, an 81-kernel CUDA Graph using Programmatic Dependent Launch (PDL) reaches 97.4% of the matched persistent megakernel's end-to-end throughput for the P32/D128 Llama-3.2-1B decode workload." The numbers: persistent megakernel 983.127 microseconds, the 81-kernel chain 1,009.711, and a plain chain without dependent launch 1,169.071 ([pdl-megakernel-reconstruction](https://github.com/tie-pilot-qxw/pdl-megakernel-reconstruction)). The chain "finishes within 2.704% of the persistent path". Same compute code, 81 kernels, 2.7 percent slower. The single kernel was packaging; the thing that mattered was a schedule with no bubbles, which graphs plus dependent launch mostly deliver. It is one workload on one card by one person, unanswered by Hazy in anything the research found, and it is the most useful number on this page.

**Where it shrinks.** An independent group at MIT built the same thing for larger models: "On unquantized Llama 3.1 8B, we find that our kernel achieves a 8-20% speedup over GPTFast, and up to a 61% speedup over vLLM", and at 70 billion parameters "small improvements (2%) at short sequence lengths increasing to 13% at longer sequence lengths", with the scope stated: "we target the single-GPU, low-batch setting for the Hopper family of architectures" ([FlashFormer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22758)). A compiler that generates megakernels reports "up to 1.7× lower end-to-end inference latency" ([Mirage Persistent Kernel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.22219)). And a megakernel on AMD hardware, the one paper that shows the other edge: "1.3-1.5x lower decode latency than vLLM at batch sizes 1-8", but "slower at bs=64", because its matmul "does not yet include K-splitting optimizations for the compute-bound regime", which the authors call "an inherent tradeoff of the megakernel approach" ([Fleet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15379)). Batch one at a small model is where bubbles dominate; at batch 64 the kernels are long, the bubbles are a small fraction, and the fused kernel's simpler matmul loses.

**The regime, stated once.** From a synthesis of all of these sources written for this course's author in August 2026: the win is "memory-bound, batch-1/low-batch, short context, small dense models, latency-critical", and "Real deltas vs strong fused baselines: 1.0-1.25x. The 2-18x academic headlines are baseline choice." That last sentence is week 3 day 3's PR #1241 lesson at the scale of a whole field.

**For your Mac.** Week 3 day 5 measured the Apple version of the bubble: 231 command buffers per token and a GPU busy 68 percent of the time. The megakernel answer, one persistent kernel, is not available through MLX, which issues one kernel per operation. The reconstruction's answer is: a schedule with fewer boundaries and no drain between them, which on Apple means fewer command buffers and concurrent dispatch, the two things llama.cpp and the MLX pull request did. The 97.4 percent result is the permission to stop wanting a megakernel and start wanting a schedule.

## Numbers to remember

| | |
|---|---|
| Hazy on H100 | 78% of bandwidth; 2.5x vLLM, 1.5x SGLang |
| Reconstruction | 81 kernels at 97.4% of the megakernel, 2.7% slower |
| FlashFormer | 8-20% at 8B; 2-13% at 70B |
| Fleet | 1.3-1.5x at batch 1-8; slower at batch 64 |
