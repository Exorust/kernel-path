# Week 6, day 3 · Serving kernels: FlashInfer, Marlin, Machete

**Why this day.** The kernels under every production serving engine are not the record-setting ones; they are the ones that handle ragged caches, mixed batches, and quantized weights at every batch size. This is where week 2 day 5's quantized matvec and week 2 day 4's attention become products.

## The story

**The library under the engines.** "FlashInfer is a library and kernel generator for inference that delivers state-of-the-art performance across diverse GPU architectures. It provides unified APIs for attention, GEMM, and MoE operations with multiple backend implementations including FlashAttention-2/3, cuDNN, CUTLASS, and TensorRT-LLM" ([FlashInfer](https://github.com/flashinfer-ai/flashinfer)). Its adoption list is the serving course's cast: "SGLang, vLLM, TensorRT-LLM, TGI (Text Generation Inference), MLC-LLM, LightLLM, lorax, ScaleLLM". The paper gives the numbers and the reason: "FlashInfer achieve 29-69% inter-token-latency reduction compared to compiler backends for LLM serving benchmark, 28-30% latency reduction for long-context inference, and 13-17% speedup for LLM serving with parallel generation" ([FlashInfer paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01005)). The problem it solves is not raw speed on one shape but the shapes a server actually sees: "FlashInfer tackles KV-cache storage heterogeneity using block-sparse format and composable formats to optimize memory access and reduce redundancy", with "load-balanced scheduling" that stays "compatible with CUDAGraph which requires static configuration". A serving attention kernel's input is a bag of requests with different lengths and a paged cache; the kernel generator makes a kernel per configuration and keeps it graph-capturable. That is the engineering a research kernel never has to do.

**The quantized matmul that holds its speed.** Marlin, from week 2 day 5, stated the problem: "While existing kernels achieve relatively close to the optimal 3.87x ... speedup at batchsize 1, their performance degrades quickly as the number of inputs is increased. In contrast, Marlin delivers essentially ideal speedups at all batchsizes, enabling the maximum possible 3.87x speedup up to batchsizes around 16-32" ([Marlin](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/marlin)). The paper extends the range, "batchsizes up to 16-32 can be supported with close to maximum (4×) quantization speedup, and larger batchsizes up to 64-128 with gradually decreasing, but still significant, acceleration", and gives the end-to-end figure that matters, "up to 2.8×" in vLLM ([Marlin paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11743)). How it was built is a line for week 4's loop: "the kernel's PTX assembly was extensively analyzed in NSight-Compute". The profiler that Apple lacks is in the method.

**And then the hardware changed.** Marlin was tuned for an A10, an Ampere-class card. Its successor's announcement says what happened next: "While Marlin was specifically designed for Ampere generation GPUs and struggles on Hopper GPUs (namely H100), Machete was built on top of the work highlighted in CUTLASS 3.5.1" ([Machete, Red Hat](https://developers.redhat.com/articles/2024/10/14/introducing-machete-mixed-input-gemm-kernel-optimized-nvidia-hopper-gpus)). Its numbers: "4bit Llama 3.1 70B on a single H100. In the higher user requests rates (3+ req/s), we see a geomean speedup of 29% for input token throughput and 32% for output token throughput", and 42 percent at 405 billion parameters on four H100s. The baseline in those figures is not stated in the text and should be checked against the article's charts before being quoted as "over Marlin". The story is week 5 day 5's: a kernel at the hardware limit on one generation struggles on the next, and the fix is a rewrite on the vendor's template library.

**The pattern across the three.** Each kernel is defined by a constraint the datacenter imposes. FlashInfer: ragged, paged, graph-captured attention. Marlin: quantized weights that stay fast past batch one, which is the speculative-decoding and parallel-sampling window. Machete: the same on the next chip. None of the three is the fastest possible kernel on its single best shape. All three are the fastest kernel across the shapes a server sees. The serving course measured these engines from the outside; this is what is inside them.

**On your Mac.** The Apple counterparts exist in smaller form. MLX's attention has the prefill-and-decode split from week 2 day 4 and a two-pass decode kernel for long caches, which is a paged-cache problem in miniature. The small-batch quantized matvec from week 2 day 5 is the Marlin problem, and MLX's `qmv_wide` path with its per-chip batch limit is the Apple answer, measured at 18 percent on an M5 Max. What does not exist on Apple is the kernel generator: no FlashInfer builds a kernel per cache configuration for Metal, and no public leaderboard scores one. A serving kernel for the Mac is a smaller problem than for a datacenter, because batch sizes are small and there is one user, and that makes it a tractable brief.
