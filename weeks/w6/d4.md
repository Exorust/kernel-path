# Week 6, day 4 · Linear attention on NVIDIA

**Why this day.** Week 3 day 3 showed the gated delta rule on Apple, where the register-resident scan won and the chunked algorithm lost. On NVIDIA the chunked algorithm is the whole story, and the libraries that implement it are what an Apple chunked kernel would be measured against in spirit.

## The story

**The library.** Flash Linear Attention, FLA, is a Triton library of kernels for the subquadratic layers: retention, gated linear attention, the gated delta rule, and Kimi's KDA. Its README benchmarks the chunked kernels against FlashAttention-2 "on a single NVIDIA GB200 GPU (CUDA 12.9, PyTorch 2.9.0)": at batch 1, 8,192 tokens, 96 heads, head size 128, the forward pass takes "chunk_gdn 1.265" milliseconds against "flash_attn 3.753" ([FLA](https://github.com/fla-org/flash-linear-attention)). About three times faster than attention at that length, by division; the README does not state the ratio. The method is the chunked algorithm: split the sequence into blocks, do the inside of each block as dense matrix multiplies on the tensor cores, and carry the state between blocks sequentially. It is the algorithm that was ported to Apple in week 3 day 3's PR #1241 and lost to the scan.

**The model papers, read for cost.** Gated DeltaNet's paper is honest that the kernel is a cost, not a win, against attention at short context: "Both are slightly slower than Mamba2 (2-3K tokens/sec) due to their more expressive transition matrices", and "The Transformer++ achieves the best performance in the 2K context window domain, thanks to the highly optimized Flash-Attention-2 kernel" ([Gated DeltaNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06464v1)). Linear attention wins on long context and memory, not on a short-context kernel race. Kimi Linear's paper states the design choice that halves the work: "the operator efficiency of KDA improves by roughly 100% compared to the DPLR formulation", by "reducing the number of second-level chunk matrix computations from four to two, and further eliminating three additional matrix multiplications", and the system-level result: "reducing KV cache usage by up to 75% and achieving up to 6 times decoding throughput for a 1M context", with "a faster TPOT (1.84ms vs. 11.48ms) than MLA at 1M tokens" ([Kimi Linear](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26692v1)). The 6x is a whole-architecture number at a million tokens, not a kernel number.

**The rewrite.** In 2026 the Qwen team rewrote FLA's gated-delta prefill in TileLang, a lower-level language than Triton: "FlashQLA applies reasonable operator fusion and performance optimization to the forward and backward passes of GDN Chunked Prefill, achieving 2-3× forward speedup and 2× backward speedup over the FLA Triton kernel across multiple scenarios on NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell", and it "now serves as a backend for flash-linear-attention's GDN" ([FlashQLA](https://github.com/QwenLM/FlashQLA)). The benchmark file in the repository is more specific than the README, and the difference is the lesson. On an H200, forward, against FLA: at a batch of one and 32,768 tokens, "2.93x"; at 4,096 tokens, "1.19x"; at 2,048, "1.32x"; at a batch of 8 and 4,096 tokens, "2.58x". The "2-3x" is real at long sequences and large batches and is 1.2x at the shapes a chat prompt has. Against FlashInfer's kernel the same file ranges from 0.97x to 4.73x. A kernel's headline is its best shape; its benchmark file is its truth.

**The Apple comparison, with care.** FlashQLA's forward at 2,048 tokens, batch 1, on an H200 is 0.066 milliseconds for a shape from a 397-billion-parameter model sharded eight ways. Week 3 day 3's packed scan on an M5 Max is 1.34 to 1.38 milliseconds per layer at 2,048 tokens for a 35-billion-parameter model's shape. The numbers are not comparable: different models, different head counts, a sharded datacenter part against a laptop. What is comparable is the structure. On NVIDIA the chunked form wins because the inside of a chunk is a tensor-core matmul and the chip's ridge point is in the hundreds. On an M5 the scan won in May because its state lived in registers and the chunked port's matmuls did not reach the accelerators. A Metal chunked kernel that does reach them, through the tensor ops from week 3 day 2, is the open question, and week 4 day 5's first capstone candidate.

**For the director.** Three habits from this page. Read the benchmark file, not the README. Separate kernel numbers from architecture numbers; "6x at a million tokens" is the second kind. And when a kernel wins on one platform, ask which hardware fact it depends on before porting it; the chunked form depends on cheap dense matmul inside the chunk, which is true on an H200 and was not true of the May port on a Mac.
