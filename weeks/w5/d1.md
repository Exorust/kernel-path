# Week 5, day 1 · NVIDIA architecture against Apple

**Why this day.** The kernels that made news were written for NVIDIA GPUs, and every number in weeks 5 and 6 only makes sense against the hardware. This page is the NVIDIA core and chip, each fact placed next to the Apple fact from week 1 that it replaces.

## The story

**The core.** NVIDIA's unit is the streaming multiprocessor, "roughly analogous to the cores of CPUs" ([Modal GPU glossary](https://modal.com/gpu-glossary/device-hardware/streaming-multiprocessor)). An H100 "has 132 SMs, each of which has four Warp Schedulers that can each issue instructions to 32 threads (aka a warp) in parallel per clock cycle", and "A single SM on an H100 can concurrently execute up to 2048 threads split across 64 thread groups of 32 threads each." The warp is Apple's simdgroup under another name: 32 threads in lockstep, and "Warp size is technically a machine-dependent constant, but in practice ... it is 32." The glossary makes the same point week 1 day 3 made about Apple: "Warps are not actually part of the CUDA programming model's thread hierarchy. Instead, they are an implementation detail". The reason an SM holds 2,048 threads is the same reason an Apple core holds many simdgroups: "This latency-hiding is the secret to GPUs' strengths."

**The memory inside the core, the other way up.** An H100's "shared memory capacity per SM is 228 KB, a 39% increase compared to A100's capacity of 164 KB", out of "the combined L1 cache, texture cache, and shared memory" of 256 KB ([Hopper tuning guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/hopper-tuning-guide/index.html)), with "65,536 registers per SM" ([Shankhdhar](https://cudaforfun.substack.com/p/outperforming-cublas-on-h100-a-worklog)), which is 256 KB of 32-bit registers. Compare week 1 day 2: an Apple core has about 208 KB of registers against 32 KB of threadgroup memory per threadgroup and an 8 KB data cache. NVIDIA gives a kernel 228 KB of programmer-managed shared memory per core; Apple gives 32 KB and a dynamic cache. That one difference is why the NVIDIA matmul ladder has a shared-memory rung and Apple's guide says to skip it.

**The matrix units.** NVIDIA tensor cores are "GPU cores that operate on entire matrices with each instruction", "providing 100x more floating point operations per second than CUDA Cores" ([Modal glossary](https://modal.com/gpu-glossary/device-hardware/tensor-core)). On the H100 product page the non-tensor FP32 rate is 67 teraFLOPS and the BF16 tensor rate is "1,979 teraFLOPS" with the footnote "With sparsity", so about 989 dense; a 15x ratio ([NVIDIA H100](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/)). Apple's simdgroup_matrix from week 1 is a cooperative instruction on the ordinary ALUs; the M5's neural accelerators are the first Apple unit in the same category, and Apple's "over 4x" for the M5 is the comparable jump.

**The chip.** The H100 SXM has 80 GB of memory at "3.35TB/s" ([NVIDIA H100](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/)). The B200 "doubles the tensor core throughput compared to Hopper H100 (2.25 PFLOPS vs. 1 PFLOPS for FP16/BF16)" ([FlashAttention-4 paper](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2026/file/ae8b0b5838ba510daff1198474e7b984-Paper-Conference.pdf)), and an eight-GPU system has "64 TB/s HBM3e bandwidth", about 8 TB/s per GPU by division ([DGX B200](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/dgx-b200/)). Against week 1: the M4 Max at 546 GB/s is about one sixth of an H100's bandwidth, and the base M5 at 153 GB/s about one twenty-second. The M5 Max has up to 128 GB of memory, the H100 80 GB. The Mac has more capacity and far less bandwidth; the datacenter part has far more bandwidth per byte and no more bytes than a laptop.

**The one number that explains the kernel differences.** Put the two bandwidths against the two compute peaks and the ridge points fall out. On an H100 with dense BF16 tensor cores, Gordic computes the ridge for the PCIe card as "~410" operations per byte ([Gordic](https://www.aleksagordic.com/blog/matmul)); the SXM card is higher. Week 2 day 1 derived about 27 for the M1 Max. On NVIDIA a kernel needs hundreds of operations per byte before compute is the limit; on Apple a few dozen. That is why every NVIDIA worklog in this week is about feeding the tensor cores, and why most Apple kernels in weeks 2 and 3 were about bytes.

**One measured lesson that transfers unchanged.** Gordic ran a naive matmul with the wrong access pattern on an H100 and "performance dropped from 3171 GFLOP/s to just 243 GFLOP/s — a 13× slowdown" ([Gordic](https://www.aleksagordic.com/blog/matmul)). Week 2 day 2's transpose on Apple lost 5x for the same reason. Coalescing is hardware-independent.

**Vocabulary table.** Keep it open for the rest of the week.

| NVIDIA | Apple | Note |
|---|---|---|
| SM | GPU core | 132 on an H100; 10 to 80 on M-series |
| warp | simdgroup | both 32 threads |
| thread block | threadgroup | NVIDIA up to 1,024; Apple up to 1,024 |
| shared memory | threadgroup memory | 228 KB per SM; 32 KB per threadgroup |
| HBM | unified memory | 3.35 TB/s on H100; 153 to 614 GB/s on M5 family |
| tensor core | simdgroup_matrix, M5 neural accelerator | dedicated unit vs cooperative instruction |
