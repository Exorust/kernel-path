# Week 3, day 4 · Expert paging and MoE on Apple

**Why this day.** Mixture-of-experts models are the largest models anyone runs on a Mac, and they stress the two things Apple silicon has in a different balance from a datacenter GPU: a lot of memory, and a fast SSD next to it. The speedups here come from treating the disk as a memory tier and from kernels that stop doing work for experts that got no tokens.

## The story

**The memory problem.** A mixture-of-experts model keeps hundreds of expert weight blocks but uses a few per token. On a datacenter GPU all of them sit in HBM. On a Mac a 180-billion-parameter-class model in 4 bits is about 97 GB, which is the whole memory of a 128 GB machine with nothing left.

In August 2026 Eyal Toledano published a sequence of results on a Qwen3.8 model that is the clearest public account of the problem being attacked. First, pruning and relocation: "REAP pruning cut the expert pool from 68gb to 35gb" and "the 51b n-gram table now lives on the ssd instead of ram", giving "39gb resident, 28 tok/s decode, 600 tok/s prefill. Logits identical to the ram-resident version in every way", with "humaneval scoring 91.5% with 288 experts vs. 93.9% for stock" ([Toledano, 2026-08-27](https://x.com/EyalToledano/status/2092959261684846861)). Then the step that matters: "I stored 60% of experts on disk which stream to memory on-demand. Similar technique as n-gram streaming / Just eval'd with FULL experts (no prune) running on just 37gb of memory and decoding at 40 tok/s on my M4 Max" ([2026-08-28](https://x.com/EyalToledano/status/2093429897188299113)). His article gives the full table for the unpruned model: "48gb → 39 tok/s", "37gb → 40 tok/s ← sweet spot", "26gb → 19 tok/s", and the conclusion: "pruning turned out to be unnecessary" ([pMLX article, 2026-08-29](https://x.com/EyalToledano/status/2093605671845470644)).

Read that table as a roofline reader. At 37 GB, with 40 percent of experts in memory and 60 percent on the SSD, decode was as fast as at 48 GB with everything resident. The experts a token actually needs were in memory often enough that the disk reads hid behind the rest of the step. Below that point the misses show: at 26 GB speed halves. The disk became a memory tier with a working-set knee, the same shape as every cache.

**The second lever and how the two interact.** Multi-token prediction drafts several tokens and verifies them in one pass. Verification is the small-batch quantized matvec from week 2 day 5, and in stock runtimes it costs about as much as generation: "in most runtimes the verification loop is about as expensive as generation, which negates the MTP speedup", so it "provides little benefit on anything below an M5". His engine owns the whole forward pass, so "checking the drafter's guesses now costs barely more than a single step", and on an M4 "decode went from 26 to 37 tok/s" at 39 GB, "32 to 65 tok/s" at 73 GB. The two levers fight: "Below ~40% of experts resident, the MTP checking step touches 3-4x the experts per token and thrashes the paged tier". Verifying four draft tokens touches four tokens' worth of experts, which pushes the working set past the knee. Every number on this page is one author's report with no outside reproduction, and he says so: "take these with a grain of salt". The structure of the argument is what to keep.

**The kernel side.** Both engines attack the same inefficiency: experts receive few tokens each. In llama.cpp, "`kernel_mul_mm_id` works on 32 routed token rows at a time. An expert often receives fewer than 32 rows, but the unused rows are still multiplied and discarded", and "A 256-expert top-8 model therefore averages 8 rows per expert at `-ub 256` and 16 at `-ub 512`". Skipping the empty half of the tile gave up to 18 percent on an M5 ([llama.cpp PR #28301](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28301)). A week later the maintainer fused the routing itself, "SOFT_MAX + ARGSORT + GET_ROWS with optional norm/scale", for 1.07x on an M2 Ultra ([PR #28948](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28948)). In MLX, the expert matvec `gather_qmm` was reworked for 1.17 to 1.44x on a 397-billion-parameter model ([MLX PR #4572](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx/pull/4572)), with a line in the description worth noticing for week 4: "Given #4567 I asked Claude code to do the same for `gather_qmm`." A maintainer directed an AI to port one kernel's improvement to its neighbour, and merged the result.

**The still-open one.** MLX issue #4509, open on 2026-10-08: "gather_qmm at M=1 streams 4-bit expert weights at ~half the bandwidth of M=4 (MoE decode)", where "the routed-expert matmuls are ~40% of each decode step", with a measurement on an M1 Max of "0.752 ms | 151 GB/s" at M=1. A decode kernel running at half its bandwidth on the operation that is 40 percent of the step is a week 4 capstone candidate.
